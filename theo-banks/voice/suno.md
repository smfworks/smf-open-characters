# Voice and Suno

Theo's voice is invented. Do not describe it as sounding like a named singer.

## Voice prompt

Copy as a Suno style or persona prompt:

> Warm rhythmic male near-baritone, late 20s, close-mic funk-pop timbre with a grin in the phrasing, clear American diction without a cartoon accent, punchy and friendly, made for upbeat promos and food ads, never snarled, never a Broadway belt. Tight groove, dry room with a little analog warmth, light harmony doubles on the hook, no auto-tune, no glossy robot sheen.

Shorter alternate:

> Warm late-20s near-baritone, funk-pop grin, close and punchy, promo-friendly, rhythmic, no auto-tune.

## Song directions

Use the voice prompt above, then pick one direction. Keep lyrics in first person or close third, late-night food-truck imagery, no real people's names.

### 1. Window's open

Fifteen-to-twenty-second promo sting. Dry drums, a clipped wah guitar, and a fat bass hook. He calls the window open and names the special once. Tempo around 108–112 BPM. Hard stop at the end, no fade.

### 2. Hotter than it looks

Upbeat 30-second product ad. Funk-pop with handclaps on two and four, bright horn stabs or a synth brass stab, and a sung hook about salsa that is hotter than it looks. Tempo around 118 BPM. Smile in the vocal, tight edit points at 8, 16, and 30 seconds.

### 3. Late Plate jam

A mid-tempo late-night truck jam for a longer cut or a behind-the-window montage. Electric piano, rounded bass, light percussion, a little tape hiss. Tempo around 102 BPM. The vocal stays conversational, almost spoken on the verses, sung on a short refrain.

### 4. Sodium lamp Saturday

Radio-ready funk-pop. Live-sounding drums, a clean guitar chicken-scratch, and a rising chorus about the yellow light over the window and the last torta of the night. Tempo around 114 BPM. Good for a 60-second spot or a social loop with a chorus first.

## Lyric seed (optional)

A first line that fits all four directions:

> Flip it once, point it at you — the window's open, come get yours.
