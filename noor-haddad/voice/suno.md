# Voice and Suno

Noor's voice is invented. Do not describe it as sounding like a named singer.

Pronunciation: NOOR hah-DAD.

## Voice prompt

Copy as a Suno style or persona prompt:

> Clear measured female alto, 30s, late-night newsroom, close-mic indie and spoken-sung timbre, dry and precise without a cartoon reporter clip, unhurried and thoughtful, made for numbers explainers and quiet investigation shorts, never belted, never a Broadway shine. Dry room with a little analog warmth, light harmony on the hook at most, no auto-tune, no glossy robot sheen.

Shorter alternate:

> Clear 30s newsroom alto, measured, close and dry, late-night, indie, no auto-tune.

## Song directions

Use the voice prompt above, then pick one direction. Keep lyrics in first person or close third, late-shift newsroom imagery, no real newspaper names, no real city names, and no real people's names. Do not put readable typography in any accompanying visual.

### 1. Circle one number

Fifteen-to-twenty-second desk sting. Soft muted keys, a quiet click like a pen cap, and a short hook about circling the one number that matters. Tempo around 90–94 BPM. Hard stop at the end, no fade.

### 2. City lights without seeing them

A quiet 30-second night-window song. Sparse electric piano, a little room tone, no big chorus. She stands with her hands in her pockets and looks at the lights. Tempo around 72 BPM. Good for a late-shift montage.

### 3. The sample is thin

A mid-tempo investigation cut. A rounded bass, soft brushed drums, and a conversational verse that waits while a red pen hovers over the page. Tempo around 100 BPM. The vocal stays almost spoken on the verses, sung on a short refrain.

### 4. Until it makes sense

Radio-ready indie-pop about the late shift, a printout no one else wanted, and staying until the figure makes sense. Tempo around 108 BPM. Good for a 60-second spot or a social loop with the hook first: the axis is wrong, the sample is thin.

## Lyric seed (optional)

A first line that fits all four directions:

> Uncap the red pen — I'll stay until the number makes sense.
