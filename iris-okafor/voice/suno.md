# Voice and Suno

Iris's voice is invented. Do not describe it as sounding like a named singer.

## Voice prompt

Copy as a Suno style or persona prompt:

> Warm cinematic-ballad female alto, mid-40s, unhurried and close-mic in the verse like someone talking to a room in the dark, a round, full chest voice that opens into a wide, steady chorus, clear, warm diction, never a pop belt, never a whispery breath effect. Piano, low strings, soft timpani or floor tom, a little hall reverb, no auto-tune, no glossy robot sheen.

Shorter alternate:

> Mid-40s warm alto, cinematic ballad, close in the verse, full and steady in the chorus, piano and low strings, no auto-tune.

## Song directions

Use the voice prompt above, then pick one direction. Keep lyrics in first person or close third, with dome, star, and old-light imagery. No real people's names, no real planetarium, observatory, or telescope names.

### 1. Lights down

A 15–20 second promo sting for a science or education spot. A single held piano chord, a soft low-string swell, and one sung line as the house lights go down. Tempo around 70 BPM. Ends on a held note with a short room tail, no fade.

### 2. Light that left before us

A 60–90 second cinematic ballad for a longer explainer or a documentary closer. Piano verse, strings entering on the second verse, a broad chorus about light that left the star before anyone in the room was born. Tempo around 72 BPM. Builds once and resolves quietly.

### 3. Tuesday matinee

A gentle 30-second homeschool or classroom cut. Light piano and celesta, a soft brushed kit, a warm singable refrain kids can follow. Tempo around 96 BPM. Clean edit points at 8, 16, and 30 seconds.

### 4. One more season

A slow late-night ballad for a behind-the-scenes montage: the projector lamp, the empty seats, the console glow. Felt piano, a cello line, faint room hum. Tempo around 64 BPM. The vocal stays conversational until a single lifted last chorus.

## Lyric seed (optional)

A first line that fits all four directions:

> Click it off, look up — that light was traveling long before we came.
