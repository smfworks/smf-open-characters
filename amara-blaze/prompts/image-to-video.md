# Image-to-video

House rule: one action, or one short two-beat action, and one camera move per shot. Start from a first-frame still. Wardrobe, tools, and jewelry that start in the frame stay as they started. Identity is locked to `refs/01-headshot-front.png`. Photoreal, candid, no beauty sheen.

The tattoo, if visible, stays a small faded outline triangle on her left side of the neck (photo-right in a front view) and does not jump sides, morph, or appear on the right side of the neck (photo-left in a front view). If a turn would place it incorrectly, hide it rather than flip it. The tattoo-free fallback is a green butterfly hair clip on the left side of her hair; do not invent or move a wrong-side tattoo. Gold hoops stay in the ears that already show them. Hair stays fire-red / bright orange-copper and jaw-length. Eyes stay vivid natural green. Outfit varies; do not force the grey sweater.

Props stay unprinted: no readable text, logos, or typography on diner signage, album covers, posters, or a record label. The carafe, mug, tonearm, needle, and record stay what they are and do not morph.

On-camera tell: She drops the needle on a record and waits a beat for the first note.

If the tell is a two-beat, the needle must land, then she waits, then a small closed-mouth smile can land. Both hands stay visible for the whole shot; after she leaves the tonearm, the right hand must remain in frame, resting on the platter edge or the table. Do not add a third beat (no look-to-camera on the tell, no flip of the record, no speech).

## Suggested proof shots

### Shot A — The diner pour (from `06-diner-shift`)

- **First frame:** `refs/06-diner-shift.png`
- **Action:** She continues pouring coffee from the carafe into the mug. One beat. The carafe stays a carafe; the mug stays a mug.
- **Camera:** Slow push-in.
- **Duration:** about 5 seconds (the library proof trims this shot to 4.0s).
- **Prompt sketch:** First-frame lock. Amara continues the pour. Identity locked: fire-red jaw-length hair, vivid natural green eyes, tattoo on her left side of the neck (photo-right in this view). Exactly two arms and two hands, five fingers each. No readable diner text. Photoreal, no beauty sheen. Native ambient diner sound only, no music.
- **Check:** count fingers on the carafe handle and the mug; confirm the tattoo stays on her left / photo-right and does not drift; confirm no readable signage.

### Shot B — Lake Michigan wind (from `07-lake-michigan-wind`)

- **First frame:** `refs/07-lake-michigan-wind.png`
- **Action:** Wind moves her hair as she looks out at the water. Hands stay in her pockets. No other action.
- **Camera:** Almost still, or a very slow push.
- **Duration:** about 5 seconds.
- **Prompt sketch:** First-frame lock. Wind in the fire-red hair and rust scarf; her gaze eases toward Lake Michigan. Hands stay in the coat pockets. Exactly two arms. The tattoo stays on her left side of the neck (photo-right in this view) or is hidden by hair or scarf; it is never flipped. Photoreal, identity locked. Native ambient wind and waves only, no music.

### Shot C — The record-shop tell (from `09-record-shop`)

- **First frame:** `refs/09-record-shop.png`
- **Action:** She lowers the needle onto the record, waits a beat, and a small closed-mouth smile lands. After the drop, the right hand leaves the tonearm but stays fully visible in frame.
- **Camera:** Almost still, or a very slow short push-in.
- **Duration:** about 5 seconds (the library proof trims this shot to 4.0s, ending on the landed smile).
- **Prompt sketch:** First-frame lock. Amara lowers the needle, waits, then a small closed-mouth smile lands. Exactly two arms and two hands the whole time, each hand always visible, five fingers each. The tonearm stays a tonearm; the record stays a record. No readable album, poster, or label text. Photoreal, identity locked, tattoo stable on her left / photo-right. Native ambient shop sound only — a quiet room, the soft needle drop — no score.
- **Check:** both hands remain traceable after the drop; do not ship a take where the right hand vanishes into the sleeve or leaves the frame.
