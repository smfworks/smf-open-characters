# Voice and Suno

Amara's voice is invented. Do not describe it as sounding like a named singer.

Pronunciation: uh-MAR-uh BLAZE.

## Voice prompt

Copy as a Suno style or persona prompt:

> Warm conversational female voice, 24, Michigan, close-mic folk-pop and slice-of-life timbre, a warm Midwest clip with a slightly nasal quality, clear American diction without a cartoon accent, friendly and unhurried, made for diner spots and quiet indie shorts, never belted, never a Broadway shine. Dry room with a little analog warmth, light harmony on the hook at most, no auto-tune, no glossy robot sheen.

Shorter alternate:

> Warm 24-year-old Midwest voice, slightly nasal, conversational, Michigan, close and unhurried, folk-pop, no auto-tune.

## Song directions

Use the voice prompt above, then pick one direction. Keep lyrics in first person or close third, West Michigan diner and lake imagery, no real people's names.

### 1. Fresh pot

Fifteen-to-twenty-second diner sting. Soft brushed drums, a clean electric guitar, and a short hook about putting a fresh pot on. Tempo around 92–96 BPM. Hard stop at the end, no fade.

### 2. Colder than it looks

A quiet 30-second lake song. Acoustic guitar, a little wind in the room, no big chorus. She says the lake is colder than it looks. Tempo around 78 BPM. Good for a Sunday turnout montage.

### 3. Dollar bin

A mid-tempo used-record-shop cut. Soft vinyl crackle, a rounded bass, and a conversational verse that waits a beat for the first note. Tempo around 100 BPM. The vocal stays almost spoken on the verses, sung on a short refrain.

### 4. Open-to-two

Radio-ready folk-pop about the open-to-two shift, a Civic with a cracked dash, and the drive back to the water. Tempo around 108 BPM. Good for a 60-second spot or a social loop with the hook first: walnut pie is better than the cherry.

## Lyric seed (optional)

A first line that fits all four directions:

> Wait a beat for the first note — I'll put a fresh pot on.
