Prepend the identity block in `../character-block.md` to every prompt, or start from the locked headshot as a reference image and keep the block in the prompt as well. The front headshot is the identity lock. Generate it first, then use it as the reference for views 02–09.

Photography, every view: a candid real photograph, mixed 35mm documentary and phone-camera looks, harsh uneven practical forge and barn light, soot, sweat, a messy working shop, sensor noise, imperfect focus, moderate pores, no catalog sheen, no beauty sheen, no sparkly catchlights, no CGI. Fictional original character, not a likeness of any real person.

Visible locks must hold: thinning sparse silver-white hair with scalp visible, like the locked headshot, no hat; a short pale nick-scar on the right cheek just above the beard line (photo-left in a front view) — hide it in a left-side view, never flip it to the left cheek; a faded pale thumbprint burn scar on the outer right forearm just below the rolled sleeve only (photo-left in a front view, photo-right in a rear view), never on the left arm. If a one-sided mark cannot be placed correctly, hide it rather than flip it. Wardrobe: worn brown leather farrier's apron, faded indigo chambray with sleeves rolled to mid-forearm, heavy dark-brown canvas work pants, scuffed chestnut work boots, rust shop rag in the apron pocket. Props stay unprinted: no readable typography or logos on tools, drawers, walls, or signs.

The mouth stays closed and neutral unless a prompt clearly asks for a small closed almost-smile. Count exactly two arms and two hands, each traceable from its own shoulder, five fingers each, at full size before accepting a frame.
