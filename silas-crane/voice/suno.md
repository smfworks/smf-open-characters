# Voice and Suno

Silas's voice is invented. Do not describe it as sounding like a named singer.

Pronunciation: SY-lus KRAYN.

## Voice prompt

Copy as a Suno style or persona prompt:

> Weathered unhurried male baritone, 60s, Appalachian, close-mic country and craft timbre, a dry working voice with gravel at the edges, clear American diction without a cartoon mountain accent, unhurried and spare, made for heritage cuts and quiet workshop shorts, never belted, never a Broadway shine. Dry room with a little analog warmth, almost no harmony, no auto-tune, no glossy robot sheen.

Shorter alternate:

> Weathered 60s Appalachian baritone, unhurried, close and dry, country and craft, no cartoon accent, no auto-tune.

## Song directions

Use the voice prompt above, then pick one direction. Keep lyrics in first person or close third, Blue Ridge forge and barn imagery, no real town names and no real people's names.

### 1. Last tap

Fifteen-to-twenty-second forge sting. A low acoustic guitar, a little anvil click as percussion, and a short hook about finishing the last tap. Tempo around 76–80 BPM. Hard stop at the end, no fade.

### 2. Light off the ridge

A quiet 30-second dusk song. Sparse acoustic guitar, a little night insect room, no big chorus. He watches the light go off the ridge with a rag in his hand. Tempo around 70 BPM. Good for a barn-door montage.

### 3. Coal is burning clean

A mid-tempo work cut. Soft bellows and a rounded bass, a conversational verse that waits for two light taps on the horn before the line comes in. Tempo around 88 BPM. The vocal stays almost spoken on the verses, sung on a short refrain.

### 4. Leave it by the door

Radio-ready Americana about shoes for the neighbors, a coal forge behind the barn, and a broken hinge left by the door. Tempo around 96 BPM. Good for a 60-second spot or a social loop with the hook first: the shoe is tight enough, the coal is burning clean.

## Lyric seed (optional)

A first line that fits all four directions:

> Two light taps on the horn — then I'll tell you if the shoe is tight enough.
