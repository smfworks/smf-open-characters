# Voice and Suno

Marisol's voice is invented. Do not describe it as sounding like a named singer.

## Voice prompt

Copy as a Suno style or persona prompt:

> Warm, slightly worn female alto, mid-30s, intimate close-mic late-night radio timbre, a little smoke in the midrange, soft coastal American diction without a cartoon accent, unhurried, dry humor under the breath, never belting, as if she is speaking to one person on the other end of a ferry channel. Dry room, slight tape warmth, no auto-tune, no bright pop sheen. Alt-folk and slow-core coloring.

Shorter alternate:

> Smoky warm alto, late-night ferry radio, close and unhurried, intimate alt-folk, worn but kind, dry humor, no belting.

## Song directions

Use the voice prompt above, then pick one direction. Keep lyrics in first person or close third, harbor-night imagery, no real people's names.

### 1. Foghorn lullaby

Slow fingerpicked acoustic guitar in a minor key, a low analog organ pad, almost no drums, maybe a distant foghorn used as a single tone, not a sound-effect joke. A lullaby for the last crossing. Tempo around 62–70 BPM. Sparse, roomy, whispered-adjacent vocals.

### 2. Channel marker

Mid-tempo alt-folk with brushed snare, upright or muted electric bass, and a clean electric guitar that chimes on the off-beats. A night-shift walking song. Tempo around 96 BPM. Chorus lifts a little, but the vocal stays conversational.

### 3. Last crossing

Cinematic folk ballad. Piano and bowed cello, soft timpani or a low drum on the turns, wide but still intimate. For end titles or a long mid-video hold. Tempo around 72 BPM. Let the last refrain thin out to voice and cello.

### 4. Token once

Spare, almost spoken-word folk. Just voice, a muted nylon guitar, and a low cello drone. Built around the habit of tapping the token once against her collarbone before she talks. Short phrases, lots of air, no big chorus. Tempo free or around 58 BPM. Good for a 15–20 second sting or a cold open.

## Lyric seed (optional)

A first line that fits all four directions:

> One tap on the token, then I call you in from the weather.
